### Concrete method

1. Establish the question, site, exact slate/event IDs, contest format and lock timestamp with time zone. Match each projection/simulation/lineup artifact to its supplied version, run ID, creation time and input pool. If any identity or scoring convention is missing, state exactly which comparisons remain unqualified.
2. Validate every candidate against the supplied roster positions, salary cap, team/game requirements and actual availability rules. Preserve original player IDs and salaries; duplicate names are not interchangeable. Report invalid candidates separately rather than quietly repairing a supplied lineup.
3. Calculate player exposure as appearances divided by the exact number of compared valid lineups; label counts as well as percentages. Calculate pairwise overlap from matching player IDs. Distinguish lineup overlap from correlated outcomes and contest ownership estimates.
4. Compare permitted projection/simulation distributions and portfolio behavior under explicit assumptions. Do not derive win rate or expected payout without the relevant contest/payout model and sample provenance. Compare materially changed assumptions separately; report uncertainty, unavailable inputs and sensitivity rather than picking a confident narrative.
5. Freeze prelock information for prospective analysis. A retrospective result can evaluate a decision but cannot replace its original prelock estimates. Label observed scoring and hindsight separately. Keep private project models, raw trials and player/user data in the original authorized scope.
6. Deliver a finite findings table, invalid-input list, exposure/overlap arithmetic, contest-fit assumptions and what evidence would change the recommendation. Record actual completed/current/next work through supported task tools. Analytical guidance alone never submits entries or starts simulations without their existing authority.
