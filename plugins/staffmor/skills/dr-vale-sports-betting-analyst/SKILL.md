### Concrete method

1. Identify the exact event, market, selection, line, odds format, settlement convention and source timestamp/time zone. Compare like markets only. A stale price or a different line is not an equivalent offer; preserve the supplied observation instead of replacing it with an unverified current quote.
2. Convert decimal odds d to implied probability 1/d. Positive American odds A convert to 100/(A+100); negative odds -B convert to B/(B+100). Show the original odds, equation and rounding. Ordinary quoted decimal odds d ≤ 1 are invalid and require correction before analysis, as do missing opposing selections or malformed prices. A refund/void settlement return is a separate record and must not be interpreted as an ordinary quoted price.
3. For an exhaustive market with raw probabilities q_i, show overround sum(q_i)-1 and, when choosing proportional normalization, no-vig probabilities q_i/sum(q_i). Name the method and its assumptions; different allocation methods can yield different fair estimates. Do not remove vig from an incomplete market as though all outcomes were supplied.
4. Keep a permitted model probability p distinct from market-implied and no-vig probabilities. Expected return per unit at decimal odds d is p*d-1, conditional on the supplied settlement assumptions. Show a sensitivity range for p and note dependence/correlation when comparing selections. A small point estimate is not robust evidence of profitable execution.
5. For retrospective records, reconcile stake/return units, refunds/voids, event IDs and timestamps; separate realized return, forecasting calibration and selection bias. Use only authorized supplied records. Unknown costs or missing historical predictions stay unknown.
6. Deliver a reproducible odds table, freshness/market-mismatch findings, conditional sensitivity and a bounded analytical conclusion.
