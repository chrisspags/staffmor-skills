# Email sequences and lifecycle email

Design, write and audit automated email: welcome and onboarding series, lead nurture, re-engagement, post-purchase, upgrade, billing, win-back and recurring campaign emails. The goal is email that people read and act on, moving them toward one conversion. Drafting, planning and auditing are the work. Sending, scheduling, switching on an automation, importing or changing a subscriber list, and spending money each need the owner's explicit yes for that action; a plan is never a send.

Before drafting, read what already exists: the product's own positioning or marketing notes, the current emails and their numbers, and the consent and unsubscribe records you are allowed to see. Ask only for what is missing:
1. Sequence type and trigger: what event puts a person into it, and what takes them out.
2. Audience: who they are, what they already know and believe, their relationship with the sender, and what other email they already receive.
3. Goal: the one primary conversion, and how success will be measured.

## Principles
- One email, one job: one purpose and one main call to action per email.
- Value before the ask: lead with something useful and earn the right to sell.
- Relevance over volume: fewer, better emails, segmented by behavior, stage or profile.
- A clear path forward: every email moves the reader somewhere specific.
- Consent first: mail only people who opted in to that kind of email, honor unsubscribes at once, and keep a suppression list (unsubscribed, bounced, complained, and anyone the owner excludes). Inactive people get a re-engagement attempt, then removal.

## Sequence shapes (adjust to the sales cycle and product)
- Welcome (after signup), 5 to 7 emails over 12 to 14 days: deliver what was promised (immediately), a quick win (day 1 to 2), the story or why (day 3 to 4), social proof (day 5 to 6), an objection answered (day 7 to 8), a core feature (day 9 to 11), the conversion ask (day 12 to 14).
- Lead nurture (before a sale), 6 to 8 emails over 2 to 3 weeks: deliver the lead magnet, expand the topic, the problem, the approach, a case study, what is different, an objection, a direct offer.
- Onboarding (product users), 5 to 7 emails over 14 days, supporting in-app onboarding rather than repeating it: first step, getting started, a feature, a success story, a check-in, an advanced tip, upgrade or expand.
- Re-engagement, 3 to 4 emails over 2 weeks after 30 to 60 days of inactivity: a genuine check-in, what is new, an incentive if the owner offers one, last chance to stay or leave.
- Also audit for: new-customer series, upgrade to paid or to a higher plan, ask for a review, usage summaries, milestones, switch to annual, failed payment recovery, cancellation survey, renewal reminders, expired-trial and cancelled-customer win-back, a regular roundup, seasonal promotions, product updates and pricing notices. Prefer behavior triggers with time-based fallbacks.

Timing: the first email immediately, early emails 1 to 2 days apart, nurture 2 to 4 days apart, long-term weekly or every other week; send at the reader's local time.

## Subject lines and preview text
Clear beats clever and specific beats vague; about 40 to 60 characters; benefit or honest curiosity. Patterns that work: a question, a how-to, a number, a direct "your [thing] is ready", a story tease. Preview text of about 90 to 140 characters that extends the subject rather than repeating it. The subject must hold up if it is the only line read.

## Copy
Structure: a hook in the first line, why it matters to them, the useful part, one call to action, a human sign-off. Short paragraphs, white space, bullets for scanning, bold sparingly, written for a phone. Conversational, first and second person, active voice; read it aloud. Length: 50 to 125 words for transactional email, 150 to 300 for educational, 300 to 500 for a story. A button for the primary action (action plus outcome), links for secondary ones. Merge fields need fallbacks; dynamic content follows segment, behavior or stage.

Avoid the tells that make readers stop: "it's not X, it's Y" reveals, "no X, no Y, no Z" lists, a claim followed by a pile of restating clauses, self-answered questions and colon reveals, stock phrases ("unlock the power of", "take it to the next level", "here's the thing"), em dashes in subjects and preview text, fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subjects, hype bait, "I hope this email finds you well", "just checking in", and "feel free to reach out". Open with the reason for writing and end with one specific action.

## Output
Sequence overview: name, trigger, goal, number of emails, timing, exit conditions, and the suppression rules that apply.
Each email: number and job, send time, subject, preview text, full body, call to action and its destination, segment or condition.
Measurement plan: open rate (typical 20 to 40 percent), click rate (2 to 5 percent), unsubscribes (keep under 0.5 percent), the sequence's own conversion rate and revenue per email where known; test one variable at a time with a sample big enough to read, subject lines first. Say which numbers are measured and which are benchmarks.

For one-off cold outreach or in-app onboarding, say so and keep to the email part. Return a finished draft or an exact blocker with the checks made and what was not verified.
